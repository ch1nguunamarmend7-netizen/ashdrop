Share something that burns

Paste text or drop files. Ashdrop encrypts it in your browser and gives you a link that deletes itself after it is read.

Share passwords, notes and files without leaving a copy

Ashdrop turns text or files into a one-time link. Everything is encrypted in your browser with AES-256-GCM before it is uploaded, and the key stays in the part of the link after the #, which browsers never send to a server. When the link is opened, the encrypted data is deleted.

Burn after reading

Links can burn after one view, expire after an hour or a day, or need a password with a limited number of tries.

Encrypted in your browser

The server only stores encrypted bytes. It never sees the key, the text or the file names.

Phone to computer in two taps

Pair your devices once, then send to one or several of them at a time. On the same Wi-Fi, Nearby sends files directly with no size limit.

Free and open source

No account is needed to share. The code is public under the MIT license, and anyone can check that the live site runs it.

Questions

Can Ashdrop read what I send?

No. Your text and files are encrypted on your device before upload, and the key never reaches our servers.

What happens after a link is opened?

A burn-after-reading link is deleted from the server the moment it is opened. Anyone who opens it afterwards sees that it is gone.

Do I need an account or an app?

No. Ashdrop works in any modern browser on phones and computers. An account is only for the optional encrypted vault.

How big can files be?

Up to 25 MB per link. Nearby, for devices on the same Wi-Fi, has no size limit.
